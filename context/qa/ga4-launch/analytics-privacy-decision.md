# Analytics privacy decision — preparation only

The owner confirmed **no existing approved analytics/cookie policy or consent tool**, then explicitly selected **simple on-site analytics opt-in: GA4 starts only after Allow; declining leaves forms fully usable** on November 9, 2026. The distinction below corrects the earlier overly broad suggestion that a missing banner necessarily blocks launch. This implements the chosen measurement policy; it does not assert universal legal necessity or certify legal compliance.

- GA4 normally uses first-party cookies to distinguish visitors and sessions. Analytics-only operation still involves measurement; removing Ads does not eliminate that collection. [Google's cookie documentation](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/11397207?hl=en).
- Google Analytics requires an appropriate privacy policy disclosing relevant cookies/data collection and Google Analytics use. This is a disclosure requirement, separate from a universal popup requirement. [Google Analytics terms, section 7](https://marketingplatform.google.com/about/analytics/terms/us/).
- Whether prior permission is needed depends on applicable requirements and the implementation. Google's EU user consent policy requires consent for cookies/local storage where legally required for covered users; a Jacksonville business address alone does not answer the visitor-location question. This report does not make a jurisdiction-specific legal determination. [Google's policy](https://www.google.com/about/company/user-consent-policy/).
- Consent Mode communicates choices to Google; it does not decide the business's obligations or itself collect the visitor's choice. Basic and advanced implementations have different pre-consent behavior. Do not promise no collection before Allow unless the implementation actually blocks measurement and avoids replaying pre-consent activity. [Google's implementation guide](https://developers.google.com/tag-platform/security/guides/consent).

**Current implementation:** custom basic opt-in in the draft website. No Google measurement script loads before Allow or after initial Decline. Pre-consent application events are dropped rather than queued for later transmission. Allow starts one current-route pageview; later explicit events follow the existing accepted-only contract. Google consent commands set analytics denied by default, update only analytics to granted after Allow, and retain all advertising consent denied. Choice is browser-local, versioned and expires after 180 days; blocked storage supports the current page session and otherwise fails closed. Privacy and Analytics choices remain available at the bottom of every page, including the emergency landing page. The new `/privacy` page describes the actual implementation and existing form providers without inventing data-retention promises.

Withdrawal disables the known GA4 destination first, removes this site's standard `_ga` / `_ga_*` cookies and reloads to unload executing GTM code. The choices panel warns that unsent form text is not saved on that reload. Unrelated cookies remain. Cross-tab withdrawal also stops the loaded container. Preview host restrictions and localhost QA CSP were not changed. Form fields, submission callbacks, attribution, Turnstile/Siteverify, recipient routing and provider behavior are preserved. Nonconsenting accepted enquiries remain valid business leads but intentionally produce **no GA4 lead event**; analytics totals cover consenting visitors.
